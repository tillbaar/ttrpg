# House Rules

## General Rules

The Savage Worlds rule system supports a broad range of settings, from dark and gritty detective tales, to cinematic epics, to political thrillers. While these base rules provide a good framework for anything one wants to do, the following Setting Rules bring the world and the action to life. Setting Rules accommodate different play styles and help emulate genres or tropes of established settings. The following Setting Rules are in effect.

#### Excellency
During character creation, each player character selects one Skill and applies an Excellency to it. This possesses a narrower focus than the Skill normally covers and represents a particular application of the Skill that the character has an uncanny talent for. Whenever using a Skill in a manner described by the Excellency, the character gets one free reroll. If the character has multiple Excellencies that apply to a situation, they still only get one free reroll. Excellencies stack with Edges that grant free rerolls. At each new Rank, a character may select another Excellency. This new Excellency may be in the same Skill or a new one. A single Skill may have up to three Excellencies. There is no set list of Excellencies. Players are encouraged to get creative inventing them. However, no Excellency should apply at all times, as they represent niche specialties. For example, a Melee Excellency should not apply to a particular weapon, as that would clearly be the weapon the character would use at every opportunity. Some examples of Excellencies are:

#### Hard Choices
The GM and their Wild Card characters don't start with Hero Points. Every time the players spend one it goes into the GM's pool where it can be used for any of their characters. Jokers no longer grant Hero Points to either side.

#### Resting
Usually, the recovery of Wounds, Fatigue, and Power Points is measured in hours. This system is more abstract. Resting means the characters are not exerting themselves beyond very basic activity.

##### Short Rest
The characters spend about half an hour catching their breath. They may walk about, but they cannot travel. A Short Rest grants the following:

<ul>
  <li>
    Recharge 5 Power Points, plus any extra from Rapid Recharge or similar sources.
  </li>
  <li>
    Recover one level of Fatigue, unless the type of Fatigue specifies an extended recovery time longer than an hour or another recovery condition.
  </li>
</ul>

##### Long Rest
The characters settle down for at least six hours. This is an opportune time to set up camp, cook some food, or get a nap. They may not travel during a Long Rest unless they are being conveyed in a vehicle, such as a coach or ship. It takes a whole day to benefit from two Long Rests. A Long Rest grants the following:

<ul>
  <li>
    Recharge all Power Points.
  </li>
  <li>
    Make one Vigor roll to recover from a single wound naturally.
  </li>
  <li>
    Recover all levels of Fatigue, unless the type of Fatigue lists a specific condition.
  </li>
</ul>

## Combat Rules

#### Desperate Attack
Desperate attacks are frantic efforts to hit a target at the expense of damage. The attacker adds +2 or +4 to any Melee roll and subtracts a like amount from damage if they hit. This can be determined per attack (before rolling), and cannot be combined with a Wild Attack.

#### Limited Actions
A character may only perform one limited action on their turn, whether it’s the same or different actions. For example, an elemental may meld into or out of its element as a limited action. That means it cannot merge with its mountain and back out in the same turn. The same applies to free actions. If a cloak of teleportation allows you to teleport as a &raquo;&hairsp;limited free action&hairsp;&laquo;, you may only do so once per turn.
