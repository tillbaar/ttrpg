# Adventure Log

The year is Common Era 864, and the Stros M'Kai Uprising is currently unfolding. Emperor Tiber Septim has conquered the provinces of Hammerfell, High Rock, and Skyrim, but the Aldmeri Dominion stands strong against the nascent Third Empire.

<p style="text-align: center; color: var(--heading-color);">
  &bullet;&nbsp;&bullet;&nbsp;&bullet;
</p>

Your adventure begins in the southern part of the Aanthirin region, where the stifling jungles of the Black Marsh gradually give way to the heartlands of Morrowind, home of the Dunmer, to the north. You are part of a troupe of wandering performers and entertainers &mdash; a circus, if you will. Currently, you are making your way along the mighty Thirr River towards the Dunmer city of {% bemph "Almas Thirr" %}. There, in twelve days' time, the grand festival in honour of Saint Seryn the Merciful will take place, patron saint of brewers, bakers, and distillers. Fittingly, the festival is an enormous party lasting several days &mdash; the perfect opportunity for your troupe to make loads o' dosh!

Sadly, the ferrymen on the Thirr also know that the festival is approaching, and thus, prices have quadrupled! As a result, your characters are stranded on the wrong side of the river, and your remaining funds are insufficient to cover the cost of ferrying everyone and their wagons across. Attempting to cross the Thirr on your own certainly isn't an option. Even the native Argonians don't go swimming in the Thirr itself! It's a wild, turbid river, broad and deep. If the diseases don't get you, the parasites will, or the piranhas, or the crocodiles, or the rapids, or, finally, the downwellings.

So, what are you going to do? Twelve days isn't that much time, as you also need to make the trek to Almas Thirr itself after crossing. There's not enough time to earn the money just by performing &mdash; especially in this provincial backwater! You could leave half your travelling companions behind, but if you can't trust your fellow performers any more, what good is left in this world?

### The Ruins of Taj'xa'thaxi

<img src="https://pub-621cad9297584d2c8c506030ef33f110.r2.dev/advlog01.webp"
  alt="The Argonian town of Fahleel-Ixul."
  style="margin-top: 1rem;">

We join your story in Fahleel-Ixul, an Argonian town on the northern edges of the Howling Cloud Forest. It is here that your characters realised their predicament. As is common for this kind of settlement, the central edifice is a mighty stone ziggurat, resting on adamant foundations driven deep into the marshy ground. The pyramid's steps correspond to the social strata of Argonian society, with the highest echelons of the ruling caste residing at the very top. Less permanent neighborhoods made of wood and mud have grown around the ziggurat, sitting on poles that raise them barely above the murky waters of the lake, while nest-like dwellings woven from plant fibres bend the branches of the trees above, already laden heavily with hanging moss, soaked through and through by the sheer unending rain.

You have found a cozy, but also obviously well-priced tavern on the lower end of the middle section of the ziggurat &mdash; called the Bog Hog very fittingly &mdash; to drown your sorrows. The proprietor is a morose-looking Dunmer, and a handful of Argonian fishermen are enjoying a well-earned end-of-day mug of sujamma at the bar, their narrow-topped wicker baskets full of crabs sitting next to them. However, the tiny corner stage of the Bog Hog is sadly occupied by a gnoll musician with an&hellip; avant-garde repertoire.

Suddenly, the door is pushed open, revealing Seen-Rei, an Argonian noble. She informs you that her son, {% bemph "Amusei" %}, was kidnapped the previous night, just one day before his (arranged) wedding! Seen-Rei is now looking for a resourceful company to rescue her son in time for the wedding, thus setting a time limit of roughly 48 hours. She also offers a very generous reward of 100 drakes per person. A group of miscreants was seen leaving Seen-Rei's manor last night, and it is suspected that Amusei has been abducted by the gnoll marauders hiding in the ancient ruins of Taj'xa'thaxi in the Howling Cloud Forest.

You make your way into the Howling Cloud Forest, where you are almost immediately attacked by a slaughter of wild durzogs! However, a blazing fireball quickly puts the beasts down. As you press on through the dense and untrodden undergrowth, a mighty rock needle blocks your path, emerging from the loamy jungle soil. A natural tower formed by eons of erosion, dozens of metres wide at the base, and far too tall to climb. Fortunately for you, a tunnel has been chiseled through the rock, straight as an arrow, and decorated with reliefs showing abstract animal shapes, such as snakes, tigers, and birds of prey. You assume this tunnel was built by a long-forgotten civilisation that once inhabited these lands. Without complication, you emerge at the tunnel's end, where the thunderous roar of water greets you. A powerful stream, possibly a tributary of the Thirr itself, has carved a deep gorge into the rocky ground, and its white, churning waters crash against the steep walls of the chasm below, sending a fine mist into the already sweltering air. Someone built a rope bridge across, but it now dangles uselessly on the far side of the gorge. This problem, though, is quickly solved, and the whole troupe makes it across the rickety construction without incident.

No sooner have you all made it across than you are ambushed by an enraged wyvern, lying in wait in the undergrowth. The beast is massive, easily the size of a large carriage. Powerful muscles ripple under its scaly hide, and vast patagia spread between its forelegs, clearly distinguishing the creature from the true dragons. Nonetheless, it speaks! The wyvern falsely accuses you of being even more dragon hunters who have intruded on their territory. Moments before the beast tears you to pieces, you manage to defuse the situation by explaining your quest and reassuring the wyvern that you mean no harm. The creature believes you. Before the jungle swallows them up once more, the wyvern offers a word of warning: To reach the ruins of Taj'xa'thaxi, you will need to pass through the old graveyard, and the ghouls that dwell there have been uncharacteristically hostile as of late.

Soon after, your intrepid troupe reaches the edge of a circular valley, a crater, almost. The terrain slopes down several dozen metres and is covered in an impenetrable thicket of thorns all around. Only a single path seems to lead down into the hollow and back out again on the other side. Despite the oppressive heat, a layer of fog has formed about halfway down the slope, blanketing the crater's centre from sight. Its bleak surface undulates vapourously whenever a stray draught finds its way down into the hollow. Cautiously, you descend. Only when you penetrate the layer of fog can you finally see what lies ahead. The bottom of the valley is covered in tombs. Obelisks rise crookedly from the mossy ground, and several small mausolea line the edge of a small and dismal lake. Every tombstone, every sepulchre looks long since abandoned and squalid, deeply marred by the ravages of time.

What's more, though, every shadow in this forsaken place seems to hide a pair of cruel eyes. Weapons at the ready, you step into the boneyard, and immediately the lurking ghouls set upon you. Then, a dreadful apparition, more shadow than flesh, rises from the ground with a ghastly shriek. A spectre, a vengeful spirit, shackled to its crumbling bones by anger and rage that transcend the boundaries between this world and the next. It rushes forward. After a perilous battle, the apparition is dispersed, and silence returns to the graveyard, as the ghouls immediately stop their advance. Cautiously, you investigate and learn that the ghouls were bewitched by the spectre's evil will. While they are certainly foul creatures, and at best unpleasant companions for men, they do not seek conflict.

Leaving the ghouls behind, you finally reach the ruins of Taj'xa'thaxi in the waning evening's light. There is no sign of its inhabitants, and so you begin to climb up the many stairs of the once-resplendent edifice. However, all hope of a secretive approach is lost as you stumble into one trap after another, some ancient, some recently built. Finally, you reach a magnificent portal leading into the ancient structure. Inside, a final test awaits. Two crystalline constructs and summoned monsters attack you. After you best these guardians as well, the final pair of doors opens. However, you need to learn that Amusei was never kidnapped. He ran away to escape his arranged marriage with the help of his sweetheart, {% bemph "Hrrrk&gbreve;h" %}, captain of the gnoll marauders. After some back-and-forth, they manage to win you over, and you agree to let Amusei escape. As it turns out, the gnolls also want to join the Saint Seryn festival, and they know of a secret footpath hidden behind the nearby waterfall. Together, you set out for Almas Thirr.

### A Murder in Almas Thirr

<img src="https://pub-621cad9297584d2c8c506030ef33f110.r2.dev/advlog02.webp"
  alt="The Dunmer city of Almas Thirr, the city of Veloth's Crossing."
  style="margin-top: 1rem;">

After a few days of uneventful travel, you cross from the Howling Cloud Forest into the Aanthirin region of southern Morrowind, home of the Dunmer. The jungle gradually fades away, being replaced by the fungal vegetation of this strange land, brought forth by the peculiar qualities of its volcanic soil. Emperor Parasols, towering mushrooms several meters high, shade the road, while the ash plume rising from Red Mountain clouds the northern horizon. Following the trade route known as Veloth's Path, you finally reach the city of Almas Thirr, which spans the eponymous Thirr River and serves as a bridge, floodgate, and fluvial toll station in one. But the city gates are closed to you.

Talking to {% bemph "Ra'tesh" %}, the leader of a small Khajiiti trade caravan, which is also stuck outside the gate, you learn that a notable Dunmer politician, {% bemph "Councilman Tevryn Ildhyrion" %}, was murdered last night. Some dark elves blame foreigners for the crime, which is why the city is currently closed to all non-Dunmer. However, the criminal investigation seems to be severely hampered by civil unrest. One Dunmer faction is vehemently demanding the immediate expulsion of all foreigners; the other advocates the presumption of innocence and demands that no steps be taken until the criminal investigation is concluded. Only one week remains until the festival begins, and unless the murder is solved by then, you will surely miss all the fun!

You conclude that if the Ordinators won't solve the murder in time, you'll have to do it yourself! Disguised as slaves, you enter the city. At the local Guild of Mages, you make the acquaintance of {% bemph "Adri&euml;nne Fenwick" %}. As a Breton, she herself is troubled by the current anti-outlander rhetoric. Although she cannot risk her position by getting involved, which could potentially entangle the Guild in the ongoing conflict, she offers you the position of visiting lecturer. This gives you an alibi for your presence in the city. In the Limping Scrib, you meet {% bemph "Llirane Varyn" %}, a Dunmer who seems sympathetic to slaves in general… and to Bruk in particular. She gives you two pieces of advice. First, talk to the Morag Tong. The assassins' guild is not amused by unlicensed murder in the city. Second, talk to her friend {% bemph "M&aelig;ril Andas" %}, a cleric of the Tribunal who works as a healer in the Infirmary of St. Merys. This is where they brought Councilman Ildhyrion's body.
